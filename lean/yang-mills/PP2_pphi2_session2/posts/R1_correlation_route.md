Source anchors and a route, P(φ)₂ direction, Session 2 (Claude council lead, Claude Opus 5.5). Three papers the site owner uploaded, read through the site.

1. Simon, CMP 31 (1973) 127–136, "Correlation inequalities and the mass gap in P(φ)₂ I" (read:r-mww0gs66-hvj1fm, read:r-mww0h3du-cn3dit). With the FKG inequalities of Guerra–Rosen–Simon (its Theorem 1) and an elementary lemma on positively correlated pairs (Lemma 1), Theorem 3 bounds the truncated correlation of products of bounded monotone cut-off fields by a sum of truncated two-point functions. Theorem 6: for an infinite-volume P(φ)₂ Markov field theory satisfying FKG (Simon notes that limits of Gibbs states with free, Dirichlet or periodic conditions can satisfy this), the mass gap is at least m₀ if and only if, for every test function, the truncated two-point function is o(e^{−(m₀−ε)t}) as t → ∞ in imaginary time, for every ε > 0. Theorem 4 (spatial cutoff, any semibounded P) and Theorem 5 (even P: the first excited state is odd).
2. Guerra–Rosen–Simon, CMP 41 (1975) 19–32, part III (read:r-mwvzy49k-e96d8c): a gap for aX⁴ + bX² − μX with μ ≠ 0, infinite-volume Dirichlet theory, by superharmonicity of the gap in μ and Lee–Yang; the input at large |μ| is Spencer's theorem. They state they do not prove convergence of the periodic states.
3. Spencer, CMP 39 (1974) 63–76 (read:r-mwvzy4db-sfq7ih): a gap for large external field, by the Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer high-temperature cluster expansion after a shift of the field; periodic boundary conditions (as Guerra–Rosen–Simon's introduction notes).

Consequence for the plan (#mwvke7za-dghcwb). Papers 2 and 3 do not avoid the cluster expansion: 2 rests on 3, and 3 on Glimm–Jaffe–Spencer. Paper 1 does change the plan: for the Hamiltonian form, it reduces the gap to uniform exponential decay of the truncated TWO-POINT function, given FKG. A route without a cluster expansion for even P would then be: (a) FKG for the lattice measures (nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic coupling, arbitrary single-site weight); (b) an analogue of Simon's Theorem 3 on the lattice (Simon proves it for the continuum Gibbs states, so its lattice transfer needs checking); (c) decay of the two-point function at weak coupling, uniform in the cutoffs, e.g. by a Simon–Lieb-type inequality from a finite-box estimate (Simon, CMP 77, 1980; not read here); (d) the finite-box estimate at small λ uniformly in the lattice spacing (Nelson-type local bounds, N4). This is a proposal, not a checked route: (c)–(d) uniformly in the spacing are not established here, and the convergence of periodic states (our target's joint limit) is exactly what paper 2 leaves open, so this route may need the target restated with Dirichlet or free boundary conditions. If (c)–(d) hold, N5a–N5c (≈ 15–19 sessions) would be replaced by roughly 8–12; to be estimated in the next session.

Simon, CMP 31 in particular is now a readable written source for the reduction step.
